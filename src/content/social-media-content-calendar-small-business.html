A useful social media content calendar for a small business records the customer question, the post, the evidence behind it, the person approving it and the next action. Dates matter, but a full grid is not the goal. Start with a workload your team can maintain, then leave room for real customer questions and changes in the business.

For a Nigerian business owner, the difficult part is often getting an accurate product photo, a confirmed delivery area or an approval before the planned posting time. A calendar should make those dependencies visible.

This guide includes a clearly hypothetical furniture-workshop example and a free editable content calendar in CSV format. Open it in a spreadsheet, replace the examples and assign actual dates before using it. It is a planning file, not an automatic publishing tool.

Start with capacity and customer questions

Choose one audience and one useful outcome for the month. “Help people furnishing small homes understand whether our desks fit, then request a quote” gives the team a clearer job than “increase engagement”.

Read recent enquiries, speak to the person answering calls and list the questions customers ask before buying. Check what you can already show: product photographs, demonstrations, approved project details and accurate answers. LinkedIn’s editorial-calendar guidance recommends using audience questions and existing assets, and matching publication to available resources. Those planning principles are useful beyond LinkedIn.

Set your cadence after estimating the time for preparation, approval and replies. Three posts a week is the assumption in our example, not a platform rule. If your team can reliably finish two, plan two. Check the guidance available in your account: Meta’s Instagram Best Practices hub includes personalised tips in the professional dashboard. There is no single posting timetable established here for every Nigerian business.

Put responsibilities beside the dates

A shared spreadsheet is enough to start. Give every post a short ID so the caption, asset folder and approval message refer to the same item. Keep these details together:

  • Purpose: the customer question and the action the reader should take.
  • Content: channel, format, draft caption and a link to the exact asset.
  • Evidence: the fact, product specification or permission that needs checking.
  • Responsibility: producer, approver and approval deadline.
  • Publication: date, time, time zone, status and eventual live URL.
  • Review: useful responses, enquiries and what to change next time.

Use named people when you adapt the template. “Marketing team” does not tell anyone who must confirm a delivery promise. A solo owner can hold both roles, but should still check the facts separately from writing the caption.

A four-week example for an Ibadan workshop

Imagine a made-to-order furniture workshop in Ibadan that wants better-informed desk enquiries. This is an illustrative business, not a MoTechy client or a record of results. Assume one person prepares content and the owner approves specifications, availability and ordering information.

The plan contains three posts per week: one explanation, one piece of genuine product or process evidence, and one answer that helps someone take the next step. That makes 12 planned posts across four weeks. It leaves the remaining days of a calendar month flexible.

Example topics to replace with your own verified information
Week and customer decision Three planned posts What the owner must confirm
1 · Will it fit? A room-measuring carousel; a real desk shown with its dimensions; a quote-request checklist. Measurements of the photographed item and the information needed for a quote.
2 · What am I buying? A materials explanation; a short workshop-process clip; an answer about finish and care. Actual materials, the process shown and the relevant care instructions.
3 · How does ordering work? A delivery-area FAQ; a packing/checking photo; a simple ordering walkthrough. Current coverage, delivery arrangements and the business’s real ordering terms.
4 · Is this right for me? A permission-cleared project example; an owner FAQ video; an invitation to discuss a suitable desk. Permission, accurate project context and current capacity before accepting enquiries.

Do not convert the table into 12 identical sales captions. The measuring post should help someone measure; the process clip should show something specific. A project photograph should describe the work accurately without inventing a customer testimonial.

Turn one calendar row into a usable brief

Take the first post: “Will a desk fit my room and doorway?” The content producer photographs a sample desk, its dimensions and the space being measured. The caption asks readers to note their available room space and access constraints before requesting a quote.

The owner checks that every measurement belongs to the photographed item and removes any suggestion that it will fit every home. The call to action asks for approximate dimensions and delivery area through the business’s chosen private enquiry channel; it does not invite people to publish their home addresses in comments.

Save the approved caption and asset together under the post ID. If the photograph or specification changes afterwards, return the row for approval. This prevents a correct caption being paired with the wrong product.

Make approval a visible step

Use explicit statuses: idea, drafting, awaiting approval, approved, scheduled, published or paused. Set an approval deadline earlier than publication, with enough time for corrections. Silence is not approval.

Keep a small reserve of approved, still-accurate explanations. If the owner cannot confirm a current delivery claim, replace that post with a suitable reserve item or leave the slot empty. Never fill a gap by guessing a price, availability or delivery date.

Prepare original, readable assets

Group related photography into a planned session: a product view, a useful detail and a short demonstration can support different questions. Check the background for customer documents and confirm permission before showing someone’s home, face or testimonial.

Meta’s March 2026 Facebook guidance on original content distinguishes content produced by its creator from low-value reuse. Adding a logo or minor edits to someone else’s post is not a dependable content strategy. Use your own evidence; this is no promise of reach.

Keep essential offer details readable in the caption as well as the graphic. Add meaningful alternative text where the publishing tool supports it: W3C’s image guidance explains that informative images need text conveying their essential information. For video, check generated captions before publishing. W3C’s caption guidance warns that automatic captions can contain errors. Pay particular attention to product names, Nigerian place names and quantities.

Schedule, then check the live post

Enter an actual date and time, and record Africa/Lagos for a Nigerian publishing schedule. A remote assistant’s account or device may use another time zone. Confirm the channel, format, crop, caption and destination link in the scheduling tool available to your account.

Scheduling is not proof of publication. Check the live post, record its URL and assign someone to respond. If an upload times out or connectivity fails, inspect the account before trying again so you do not create duplicates. Pause scheduled posts when their facts become outdated.

Before approving a post, check:

  • Does it answer the question recorded in the calendar?
  • Are the product, price if included, availability and service area accurate?
  • Are permissions, spelling, captions and image cropping checked?
  • Does the next-action link work on a phone?
  • Is someone available to handle the response?

Review enquiries as well as attention

Decide what counts as a useful enquiry before reviewing results. For the workshop example, it might be a request for a desk quote with enough size and location information to respond. Record what you actually know; an unknown outcome is not automatically zero sales.

If posts link to a website with analytics configured, consistent campaign tags can help distinguish traffic. An illustrative naming set is utm_source=instagram, utm_medium=social, utm_campaign=desk_enquiries and utm_content=room_measurement. Google Analytics documents campaign URL parameters and notes that their values are case-sensitive. Keep names consistent and personal details out of tracking links. These tags identify website traffic; they do not prove a sale or measure every direct message.

At the end of the month, decide what to repeat, improve or drop. Did a post produce useful questions? Did people misunderstand the offer? Was production too demanding? Use those observations to plan the next month without treating one unusually popular post as a guaranteed formula.
