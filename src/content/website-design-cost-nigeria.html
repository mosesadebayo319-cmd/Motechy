The cost of a business website in Nigeria depends on its scope, content, functionality and support. As examples checked on 12 October 2026, Alpha Digital Network lists ₦350,000 for a standard five-page site, while Etchpoint says its website projects start at ₦450,000. These are individual providers’ advertised offers, not a national average or a MoTechy quotation.

A quote for “a five-page website” can leave more unanswered than it explains. Who writes the pages? Can the business update them? Where do enquiries go? What happens when the form fails? Does the price include moving an existing domain, retaining old search links or supporting the website after launch?

Those questions affect both the initial fee and the cost of owning the site. This guide explains how to compare website design prices in Nigeria, prepare a useful brief and calculate a first-year budget without confusing a simple company website with a custom application.

What do published prices actually tell you?

Public price lists are useful starting points, but they describe a vendor’s package at a particular time. They do not establish a standard market price. Check inclusions directly before relying on a figure, especially where a service, subscription or supplier cost can change.

Choose the type of website you need

A focused landing page

A landing page supports one offer or campaign. It may explain a service, show appropriate evidence and collect an enquiry. It can be a sensible first release when the business has a narrow objective. It still needs usable content, mobile testing and a working contact route.

A company or service website

A company site usually needs several distinct pages: services, about, selected work, contact and possibly articles. The important distinction is not simply the page count. Five pages that each require substantial research and writing can take more work than twenty pages based on one repeatable structure.

An online store

A store adds product information, stock rules, checkout, payment handling, delivery and order administration. Returns, notifications and failed payments also need decisions. The quote should describe those journeys, including who prepares product data and how the team will manage orders.

A web application

Accounts, permission levels, dashboards, approvals and business records turn the work into an application. A client portal with confidential records is a different project from a public brochure site. Our custom software guide explains how to decide whether that additional complexity is necessary.

The decisions that change the build cost

Content readiness: a developer can place your approved copy, but producing positioning, service explanations, photography and case studies is additional work unless included. Identify who owns the content and when it will be ready. Missing content can delay an otherwise straightforward build.

Design scope: adapting an established design system and creating several bespoke page layouts require different effort. Ask what is being designed, which devices are covered and how many rounds of feedback are included. The quality of the result should be judged in the actual customer journey.

Editing requirements: a content management system can make sense when staff need frequent independent updates. A small static site can suit a business with less frequent changes and an agreed update process. The platform decision should follow the people who will maintain it, rather than a blanket rule about which technology is “best.”

Integrations: payments, booking, email delivery and CRM connections need configuration, testing and ownership. Connecting a button is not always the same as verifying the complete flow. Ask what happens when the provider is unavailable or returns an ambiguous result.

Migration: replacing a live site can involve redirecting old URLs, moving content, checking forms and changing DNS records. Email may use the same domain, so changing website hosting must preserve the records needed for mail. Include migration responsibilities in the proposal.

Calculate the first-year cost

Separate the build fee from domain renewal, hosting, paid extensions, business email, maintenance and future improvements. Some providers bundle several items for the first year. Ask for the renewal amount and the terms after the introductory period.

Use the same calculation with actual proposals. Also ask what happens in year two: the original build fee may not recur, but maintenance and subscriptions can. If a provider bills in foreign currency, record the billing currency and when the conversion is determined rather than assuming a fixed naira renewal.

A maintenance agreement should define what it does. Backups, dependency updates, availability checks, small content changes and incident response are different tasks. Ask whether restoring a backup has been tested and whether major feature changes require a separate quote.

Compare proposals line by line

Give each provider a brief containing the business objective, audience, required pages, forms, integrations, available content, existing domain and expected launch date. Name the person who can approve the work. If you have a budget range, sharing it helps the provider propose a suitable scope.

Then check what the quote promises in five areas:

  1. Deliverables: page types, content, design, development and integrations.
  2. Acceptance: the customer journeys and checks required before sign-off.
  3. Ownership: domain access, hosting, source code, content and applicable licences.
  4. Support: handover, training, correction period and ongoing maintenance.
  5. Commercial terms: payment milestones, revisions, exclusions, third-party charges and how changes are agreed.

A very short quote is not automatically bad. It becomes risky when important responsibilities are left unresolved. Ask for clarification in writing and make sure the final agreement reflects the answer.

What should you test before accepting the website?

Read every page on a phone and a desktop. Check navigation, visible prices, phone numbers and important links. Try the form with missing fields, an invalid address and valid details in an agreed test. Confirm receipt with the responsible person rather than relying only on a success message.

Use the keyboard to move through controls, and check that labels and error messages explain what to do. The W3C forms tutorial explains why clear labels, instructions and feedback matter. These requirements belong in the build discussion, not as an afterthought once a customer complains.

For payments, include a suitable test environment and verify the server-side result. A browser redirect alone is not proof that an order has been paid. Paystack’s verification documentation describes checking transaction status; the implementation should also match the transaction to the intended order and amount.

Check the sitemap, page titles, descriptions and the handling of old URLs. Ask for evidence of the agreed tests. A performance score is useful information, but it does not replace trying the actual enquiry, booking or purchasing journey.

How to reduce cost without weakening the essentials

Launch the smallest version that supports the business goal. Supply approved content early, reuse layouts where appropriate and defer features that do not affect the first customer journey. A well-scoped five-page release can be expanded when you understand how customers use it.

For example, a consultancy may begin with service pages, selected work and a validated enquiry form. If it later needs customer accounts and document approvals, those requirements can become a separate application project. MoTechy’s own site and ImpactDesk illustrate these different levels of functionality.

Keep ownership, essential accessibility, reliable forms and a defined handover in scope. Removing them can leave the business with a site it cannot use or maintain. Reduce the number of features before reducing the reliability of the features customers depend on.

Website pricing questions

Does a website price include SEO?

Ask what “SEO included” means. Technical setup, titles, a sitemap and indexability checks differ from ongoing research, writing and improvement. Read our small-business SEO guide to separate the foundation from continuing work.

Do I need a custom-coded website?

Not always. Templates, website builders, content management systems and custom builds can each suit a particular brief. Compare editing needs, integrations, ownership, performance and ongoing cost before deciding.

Can I get a fixed price?

A fixed price is more useful when the deliverables and acceptance criteria are clear. Define how requests outside that scope are estimated and approved. A fixed number attached to an undefined project leaves room for disagreement.
