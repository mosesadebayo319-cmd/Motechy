A customer looking for a plumber in Abuja needs more than a business name. They need to know whether you cover their area, when someone can respond and which number to call. A Google Business Profile can help answer those questions before they reach your website.

For an eligible small business in Nigeria, the sensible order is to check for an existing listing, confirm the right location setup, enter accurate information and complete the verification Google offers. Then assign someone to keep the profile useful. This guide walks through those decisions, with an illustrative example and a downloadable setup and monthly review checklist.

First, check whether your business qualifies

Google generally requires a business to meet customers in person during its stated hours. A shop customers visit and a repair business that visits customers can qualify. An online-only business generally cannot. Having an Instagram account, a registered company or a Nigerian phone number does not, by itself, establish eligibility. Check Google’s eligibility rules against how you actually operate.

Next, distinguish three arrangements:

  • Storefront: customers visit your staffed business premises. The public address should take them to the right place.
  • Service-area business: you visit customers but do not serve them at your address. Google says to remove that address from the public profile.
  • Hybrid: customers visit your premises and you also travel or deliver directly to them. You can show an eligible storefront and a service area.

A hybrid storefront needs permanent on-site signage. Describe the places you can actually serve, rather than adding cities to look bigger. See Google’s service-area guidance. If your operation is entirely online, put the effort into a useful website and suitable marketing channels instead.

Find the existing listing and keep ownership clear

Search Google and Maps for your business name and city before creating anything. A previous employee, agency or Google’s own information sources may already have produced a listing. Follow the claim or access route shown on the existing profile; creating a second one is not a sensible shortcut around an access problem. Google explains the starting process in its verification guide.

Our recommendation is for the business to retain primary ownership through an account it controls. Give a staff member or agency the access their work needs. Google supports separate owner and manager roles, so people can work without sharing one password. Managers can handle routine edits and reviews, but cannot add or remove users. See the owner and manager permissions.

Keep a private handover record containing the profile URL, responsible person, access roles and recovery arrangements. Review access when somebody leaves. Do not store passwords or verification codes in a shared content calendar.

Prepare details a customer can rely on

Use the name your business actually uses on its signage and other materials. Do not append “best plumber Abuja” simply to insert a search phrase. Choose the fewest categories needed to describe the core business, and keep the description factual. These are part of Google’s business representation guidelines.

Before editing, prepare one reference sheet. Record the name, customer-facing phone number, website, operating hours, location arrangement and service coverage. Compare it with your website’s contact page. Differences are worth resolving before a customer discovers them.

For a shop in a plaza, check whether the map pin and entrance information will get a first-time visitor to the correct unit. For a mobile service, decide who answers the phone while the team is working. A listing that says “open” is unhelpful if nobody can explain the next available appointment.

Use a website page that answers the likely enquiry: what you do, where you work and how to request help. On a phone, follow the link and check the page loads, the contact details are readable and the enquiry route works. Update holiday arrangements when they are confirmed.

Complete the verification Google actually offers

Verification methods are selected automatically by Google. They vary by business and can include video, phone, email or mail; you cannot simply choose a different method because a tutorial recommends it. More than one check may be required. Follow the options in your profile and keep any verification codes private. Google’s current verification instructions are the reference, rather than a promise of “instant verification”.

If video recording is offered, prepare a route before starting. Google requires a continuous, unedited recording made through the profile on a mobile device, lasting at least 30 seconds. A storefront recording should demonstrate location, the business and your authority to manage it. For a service-area business, relevant surroundings, tools or equipment and management evidence matter. Keep sensitive identifiers and other people’s faces out of the recording. Read the video verification requirements before filming.

Choose a time when you can access everything you need and have a reliable connection. If verification fails, read the reason and address the missing evidence. Save a private note of what was requested and what you changed. Repeatedly creating new listings leaves the original problem unresolved.

Worked example: a mobile plumbing business in Abuja

This is an illustrative planning exercise, not a MoTechy client or a report of results. Suppose a plumber works from a home base, visits customers in Wuse, Garki and Maitama, and does not receive customers at home. The owner handles bookings, while an assistant can update the website.

Turn operating facts into profile decisions
Operating fact Practical decision Evidence to prepare
Customers are served at their premises. Use a service-area setup; do not present the home as a walk-in shop. Actual work equipment and the location evidence requested during verification.
The team covers three Abuja districts. Confirm the corresponding areas available in Google’s selector and list only supported coverage. An internal coverage list the booking assistant also uses.
Work requires an agreed appointment. Explain booking arrangements on the linked service page. Avoid a 24-hour promise the team cannot fulfil. A clear response owner and realistic working hours.
The owner controls the business account. Keep ownership with the owner; add the assistant as a manager if profile work is needed. An access record and an agreed handover process.

The same information should survive the journey from Maps to the website to a conversation. If the website invites visits to an office that is only a storage address, fix that contradiction. If the business stops covering a district, update both the profile and the booking instructions.

The useful outcome of this exercise is a coherent customer journey. It does not predict a ranking position, an enquiry count or revenue.

Use real photos and ask for honest reviews

Public profile photos should help customers recognise the business or understand its work. Google asks for clear, well-lit images that represent reality. Its photo guidance currently accepts JPG or PNG files from 10 KB to 5 MB and recommends 720 × 720 pixels. These public-photo requirements are separate from verification video instructions.

For the example plumber, a useful photo list might include maintained tools, a work vehicle if there is one, and completed work with the customer’s permission. A storefront could show its entrance and interior. Check backgrounds for private documents, customer details and addresses before uploading. This article’s cover is illustrative stock photography; your own profile should show your actual business.

After a genuine customer interaction, invite an honest review through the profile’s review link or QR code. Google prohibits incentives for posting, changing or removing reviews. See its review guidance.

A straightforward request could read: “Thank you for choosing us. If you would like to share your experience, here is our Google review link. Your honest feedback is welcome.” Do not ask customers to claim results they did not experience. Reply courteously, including to criticism, and move any discussion of private job details to a private channel.

Run a monthly check that leads to decisions

Assign one responsible person and a recurring review date. Each month, check public details, upcoming closures, website links, recent photos, unanswered reviews and the enquiry journey. Record what changed and who will handle any unfinished task. Review sooner when the business moves, changes hours or changes its phone number.

In Performance, compare equivalent reporting periods. Google defines the “calls” metric as clicks on the call button, and website clicks as clicks on the website link. Neither proves a sale or a completed conversation. Read the performance metric definitions before building a report.

For example, suppose a hypothetical monthly report shows 20 call-button clicks while staff record eight new enquiries that mention Google. Keep those as two separate observations: the records may overlap, cover different people or miss an attribution. Do not divide eight by 20 and label the result a verified conversion rate. Investigate missed calls, repeat callers and how staff record the source before drawing conclusions.

What does it cost, and will it improve rankings?

Google describes Business Profile as free. An agency may charge for its own work; ask what that fee covers, who retains access and what will be delivered. Do not confuse an agency service fee with a required Google listing payment.

Google explains local ranking through relevance, distance and prominence, and says businesses cannot request or pay for a better local ranking. An accurate profile gives Google and customers clearer information, but does not guarantee a place in the top results. Consult Google’s local ranking guidance when assessing an optimisation proposal.
