To measure marketing ROI for a small business, match recorded sales to a defined campaign, subtract the costs of fulfilling those sales and running the marketing, then compare what remains with the marketing investment. Keep the reporting period, cost definition and attribution rule visible. A revenue total alone cannot tell you whether the campaign paid its way.

A customer discovers your catering business on Instagram, asks for a menu on WhatsApp and pays by bank transfer. The advert, conversation and payment now sit in three places. Before judging the marketing, you need to connect those records without counting the same order twice.

This guide explains that process using a hypothetical Enugu office-catering business. Every amount is a teaching assumption, not a market price, recommended budget or MoTechy client result. Download the editable marketing ROI worksheet to work through your own figures.

Choose the measure before comparing results

Revenue ROAS means revenue credited to advertising divided by media ad spend. It answers how much attributed revenue you recorded per naira spent on adverts. Google’s Target ROAS documentation uses conversion value relative to ad cost. Check what your account calls “value”: an estimated lead value is not a completed sale.

Marketing ROI needs an explicit cost definition. Google’s ROI guidance includes production and advertising costs in its example. This guide uses a different, clearly labelled measure: estimated contribution-based marketing ROI, with marketing costs as the denominator.

Calculate it in three steps:

  1. Contribution: attributed net sales minus variable fulfilment costs.
  2. Contribution after marketing: contribution minus total marketing costs.
  3. Estimated marketing ROI: contribution after marketing divided by total marketing costs, multiplied by 100.

“Contribution” is the amount left after variable costs, as explained in ACCA’s cost-volume-profit guide. Our final figure is still before remaining fixed overhead and tax. It is not the company’s net profit, and attributed sales do not automatically prove that marketing caused them.

Set the reporting boundaries

Write down the campaign, offer, enquiry dates, order cut-off and attribution rule before adding numbers. For example: enquiries received during one campaign month, with completed and fully paid orders checked 30 days after that month ends. That extra window is an illustrative choice; use a window suited to your actual sales cycle.

Keep quotes, deposits for undelivered work, unpaid invoices and completed paid orders in separate columns. This worksheet uses completed, fully paid orders as a conservative operational measure, not as a replacement for your accounting revenue policy. Reconcile it with your accounts rather than treating every bank credit as a sale.

Mark a report provisional while orders, refunds or bills remain unresolved. Compare campaigns at the same stage of maturity. A completed campaign with a month of follow-up is not directly comparable with one that launched yesterday.

Include the costs that a dashboard leaves out

Net sales should reflect discounts and refunds. Keep delivery charges and related delivery costs on a consistent basis, and ask your bookkeeper how taxes collected on behalf of authorities are treated in your accounts. Use the same treatment across periods.

For the caterer, variable fulfilment costs might include ingredients, packaging, order-specific labour, delivery and payment fees. Include costs actually incurred, including unrecovered costs on refunded orders. Do not apply a blanket margin if office lunches and event orders have different economics.

Marketing costs can include media spend, photography, design, campaign management, relevant software and allocated staff time. Check whether an agency invoice already includes advertising before adding the platform bill again. Allocate shared work using a recorded rule, such as tracked hours; do not charge the whole monthly fee to every campaign.

For foreign-currency tools or advertising, use the actual naira amount charged and relevant transaction fees, with the billing date recorded. If you include an estimate for unpaid owner time, show it separately from cash expenditure. Consistency matters more than making the result look favourable.

Worked example: 6× ROAS and 33.3% estimated marketing ROI

Assume the Enugu caterer records the following totals for one defined campaign and its completed-order window. All ₦600,000 of net sales are credited to the paid campaign under its stated attribution rule; the next section tests that assumption.

Hypothetical catering campaign: assumptions, not measured business results
Item Amount What it represents
Net sales ₦600,000 Completed, paid orders after discounts and refunds.
Variable fulfilment costs ₦360,000 Costs associated with delivering those orders.
Contribution ₦240,000 ₦600,000 minus ₦360,000; a 40% contribution margin.
Media ad spend ₦100,000 Advertising charges only.
Content production ₦30,000 Production costs allocated to this campaign.
Management and staff allocation ₦50,000 Campaign work, counted once.
Total marketing costs ₦180,000 Media, content and management combined.
Contribution after marketing ₦60,000 ₦240,000 minus ₦180,000, before remaining overhead and tax.

Revenue ROAS is ₦600,000 ÷ ₦100,000 = 6×. Estimated contribution-based marketing ROI is ₦60,000 ÷ ₦180,000 × 100 = 33.3%, rounded. These figures answer different questions. The business has ₦60,000 left from the attributed orders to help cover its remaining overhead and tax.

At the same 40% contribution margin, revenue needed to cover this fixed ₦180,000 marketing investment is ₦180,000 ÷ 0.40 = ₦450,000. Against the assumed ₦100,000 ad spend, that is 4.5× revenue ROAS. This is only a marketing-cost coverage threshold for these assumptions, not whole-business break-even or a universal ROAS target. Recalculate if margins, product mix or marketing costs change.

Connect sales to marketing without overstating certainty

Create one order log with an internal order ID, campaign, source evidence, enquiry date, completion date, payment/refund status, net sales, variable costs and new-or-repeat customer status. Use that log to reconcile website orders, WhatsApp discussions and transfers. Keep unnecessary personal information out of the reporting sheet.

For website links, use consistent campaign tags. Google’s URL parameter guidance explains source, medium and campaign tracking. A campaign could use utm_source=instagram, utm_medium=paid_social and utm_campaign=office_lunch. Values are case-sensitive. These tags identify traffic in a configured analytics setup; they do not independently verify a WhatsApp order or bank payment.

Choose and document a credit rule, such as the last recorded marketing source before an enquiry. Preserve supporting evidence and leave genuinely unknown sources unassigned. A customer’s answer to “How did you find us?” can help, but is not perfect evidence. Never add platform totals together when they may claim the same order.

Google Analytics describes attribution as assigning credit to interactions. Causation is a separate question: would the order have happened anyway? Google’s Conversion Lift explanation distinguishes attributed conversions from incremental conversions, measured using treatment and control groups. A simple order log does not provide that experiment.

Test the decision with an explicitly hypothetical sensitivity check. Suppose only 75% of the caterer’s attributed sales were additional, and variable costs scaled proportionally. Incremental sales would be ₦450,000, variable costs ₦270,000 and contribution ₦180,000. After the same ₦180,000 marketing cost, the incremental return would be zero. The 75% is an assumption, not a measured estimate. It shows why a favourable attribution report alone cannot prove incremental profit.

Handle repeat sales and organic work consistently

Track customers acquired in the same period as a cohort, then report their observed repeat purchases within a stated follow-up window. Deduct repeat-order fulfilment and additional retention costs. Do not count a repeat order in two acquisition campaigns or describe hoped-for future purchases as realised lifetime value.

Organic marketing can use the same contribution-based calculation if its costs are recorded. Posts still require time, production and management. With no media spend, revenue ROAS is not applicable. If total marketing cost is zero or unknown, this ROI ratio cannot be calculated meaningfully; show the missing cost information instead of an infinite return.

Use a monthly review to make a specific decision

There is no single “good marketing ROI” that fits every small business. Required contribution depends on overhead, capacity, payment timing and the confidence of the evidence. Before changing the budget, check:

  • Completeness: are refunds, outstanding orders, fees and staff costs recorded?
  • Reconciliation: does each credited sale have one order ID and supporting evidence?
  • Comparability: are the window, cost definition and attribution rule consistent?
  • Economics: do the actual orders leave enough contribution after fulfilment?
  • Capacity: can the business deliver more orders without changing those costs?
  • Decision: should you fix tracking, adjust the offer, reduce waste or test a limited budget change?

If enquiries rise but paid orders do not, inspect response times, qualification and the offer before treating lead volume as financial return.
