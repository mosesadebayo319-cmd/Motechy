A workable digital marketing plan starts with one offer, one audience and one measurable customer action. Choose a channel that matches how customers buy, give them a clear place to enquire, and review enquiries and sales alongside reach. You can add more channels once that first journey works.

Imagine an Abuja training company preparing its next weekend course. Its Instagram looks active, its website lists several programmes, and a staff member answers WhatsApp messages between other tasks. Yet nobody can say which activity brings suitable applicants. That is a hypothetical example, but it illustrates a practical planning problem: each channel is busy, while the customer’s next step remains unclear.

A useful digital marketing strategy for a small business in Nigeria should answer five questions: what are we selling, who is likely to buy it, where will they discover us, what information will help them decide, and how will we handle the enquiry? This guide turns those questions into a plan you can run with a small team.

1. Define the offer before choosing the channel

Start with a service or product you can deliver reliably. “We offer professional training” leaves the customer to work out whether the programme fits. “A six-week evening Excel course for office administrators, with practical assignments and a published timetable” gives them something to assess. The specific version also makes it easier to write an advertisement, build a landing page and answer questions.

Write a short offer statement with the intended customer, the task they want to complete, the deliverable, the price or quotation process, and any conditions. Include what the offer excludes. A cleaning service might cover offices within a defined service area, while charging separately for upholstery or specialist equipment.

Then choose one primary action: request a quote, book an assessment, buy a product or apply for a course. These actions need different information. A quote form may ask about location and scope; a checkout needs delivery details and payment confirmation. Avoid making every page ask visitors to do everything.

2. Collect the questions customers actually ask

Review recent enquiries, sales conversations and objections. Remove personal information before using those notes for content planning. Group questions by what they reveal: uncertainty about cost, suitability, delivery, credibility or the process after payment. These are useful topics because they are tied to a decision.

For the training example, prospective learners might ask whether a laptop is required, whether classes are recorded and what they should already know. A generic motivational post answers none of those questions. A clearly explained timetable or a sample exercise may be more useful at that point in the journey.

Search suggestions and competing pages can help you discover wording, but they do not prove how many people search for a phrase. Use your own customer language alongside search research. If you have Google Search Console data, check which queries already bring relevant impressions and clicks before creating another page on the same subject.

3. Choose channels around buying behaviour

A starting point for channel selection
Customer situation Channel to consider What to prepare
They are actively looking for a nearby provider Local search and an eligible Google Business Profile Accurate service information, location details and a useful service page
They need to see the product or process A visual social platform Clear photos, demonstrations, prices or an explained quotation process
Several people must approve a business purchase Search, professional content and direct enquiries A capabilities page, relevant evidence and answers about delivery
They already know the business and need help deciding Opt-in email or a customer-initiated message Relevant answers, a clear next step and someone responsible for responding

Choose one discovery channel and one reliable contact route first. An online-only business should check eligibility before creating a Google Business Profile: Google generally requires in-person contact with customers, with specified exceptions. Read the official eligibility guidance instead of inventing an office address.

WhatsApp can be a useful contact route when your customers prefer it. The WhatsApp Business app supports a business profile, product information and customer messaging. Set clear response hours and keep essential service information on your website too, so a visitor does not have to begin a chat to understand the basics.

4. Make the enquiry journey work before buying traffic

Follow the path as a new customer on a phone. Open the post or advertisement, visit the destination, find the price or process, and attempt the intended action. Check whether the information stays consistent. Advertising an evening course and sending people to a homepage with no timetable creates unnecessary work for the visitor.

A service landing page should explain the offer, who it suits, the location or delivery format, credible evidence, common questions and one primary action. Use genuine project examples with permission. If an example is a demonstration or independent product, identify it clearly rather than implying a client result.

Test what happens after submission. Does the enquiry arrive? Who receives it? What happens outside working hours? What does the visitor see when delivery fails? A working form should show an honest result and a recovery option. A page view or button click does not establish that the business received a lead.

Assign an owner for enquiries and agree how quickly the team can realistically respond. Keep a simple record of the enquiry source, need, qualification, next step and outcome. Collect only the information needed to manage the conversation, and limit access to that record.

5. Build content around a decision, not a posting quota

Choose a weekly customer question, then answer it in the formats you can produce well. A training company could use one week to explain course suitability: a short demonstration, a prerequisite checklist and an answer to a common question. Each piece should lead to the relevant course information.

A workable content brief has six fields: audience, question, key answer, evidence, format and next action. Add an owner and approval date. This is enough structure to stop a calendar filling up with attractive posts that nobody can explain.

Use a cadence the business can maintain. Three useful posts may be appropriate for one team; another may have the material and staff for more. There is no universal posting ratio that guarantees sales. Use our seven-day content starter for prompts, then adapt the frequency to your resources and the responses you receive.

6. Connect the budget to a business result

Separate management fees, content production, advertising spend and software costs. If one invoice combines them, ask for the allocation. Otherwise, an apparent rise in cost per enquiry could simply reflect a change in what the calculation includes.

Use a small set of defined measures: qualified enquiries, sales, acquisition cost and contribution after delivery costs. Define “qualified” before counting it. For a business service, that could mean a genuine need, a serviceable location and a realistic purchasing timeframe.

Worked example — illustrative, not a MoTechy campaign result: ₦120,000 of advertising produces 30 enquiries. Twelve meet your qualification criteria and three become customers. Advertising cost is ₦4,000 per enquiry, ₦10,000 per qualified enquiry and ₦40,000 per customer. If management and creative cost another ₦90,000, the combined acquisition cost is ₦70,000 per customer. These figures describe different things; label the one you report.

Compare acquisition cost with contribution, not revenue alone. A ₦100,000 sale with ₦65,000 in variable delivery costs leaves ₦35,000 before acquisition and fixed overhead. Spending ₦70,000 to win that sale would require a defensible repeat-purchase model or a change in the economics.

Campaign parameters can help distinguish website visits from different links. Google explains the use of source, medium and campaign parameters in its campaign URL guidance. Keep naming consistent and leave personal information out of URLs. Attribution is imperfect, so also ask customers how they heard about you.

A practical first 30 days

  1. Week one: clarify. Choose the offer, collect customer questions and agree the primary action. Record a baseline of current enquiries and sales.
  2. Week two: repair. Update the service page, contact route and response process. Test the full journey on a phone and with a keyboard.
  3. Week three: publish. Release a small set of content addressing one buying question. If you run paid promotion, use a budget you can afford to test and agree a stopping point.
  4. Week four: review. Look at qualified enquiries, outcomes and recurring objections. Change the weakest part of the journey before adding another platform.

Questions business owners ask

Can I start without paid advertising?

Yes. You can improve service information, answer buying questions, strengthen relevant organic discovery and respond consistently to enquiries. Paid advertising is a distribution option. It does not remove the need for a clear offer or a working contact process.

Should I use Instagram, Google or LinkedIn?

Choose based on the customer and the purchase. A visually assessed product, an urgent local service and a complex organisational purchase have different discovery patterns. Start with evidence from your own enquiries, then test a focused channel.

How long should I give the plan?

Agree a review period that reflects your sales cycle and budget. Thirty days can reveal broken journeys and useful questions; it may not establish the long-term return from search content or a business purchase that takes months.
