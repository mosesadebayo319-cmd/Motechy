A landing page checklist for service businesses should connect the promise that brought someone to the page with the information and next step they need. Check the offer, service area, credible evidence, mobile layout, enquiry form and follow-up. A persuasive headline cannot compensate for an unclear service or a request that never reaches the business.

Suppose a Port Harcourt office manager clicks an advert for workplace cleaning. The page opens with “Complete lifestyle solutions”, a large stock photograph and a button labelled “Get started”. The visitor still does not know whether the company cleans offices, covers their location or needs a site visit before quoting.

This guide develops that hypothetical example into a practical review. It is not a client case study or a claim of higher conversion rates. Download the editable landing page review checklist to brief a copywriter, designer or developer.

Define the job of this particular page

Write one sentence before choosing a layout: “This page helps this customer understand this offer and take this next step.” For the fictional cleaning business, the job is to help an office manager request a quotation for recurring workplace cleaning in the area the company actually serves.

Distinguish requesting a quote, arranging an assessment and confirming a booking. They require different information and commitments. A visitor should not discover after pressing “Book now” that the business has neither checked availability nor agreed a price.

A dedicated campaign page is useful when an advert makes a specific promise. An existing service page may already do the job if it answers the same question clearly. You do not automatically need a new domain, separate website or nearly identical pages for every neighbourhood.

Match the advert, headline and next action

Put the advert and proposed page beside each other. Does the page name the same service, customer, location and offer? Google's landing page guidance recommends matching the destination to the advert and its call to action, with useful content and straightforward navigation.

For our example, “Office cleaning for small workplaces in Port Harcourt” is more informative than “Experience excellence”. The supporting text should explain the request: share your location, approximate office size and preferred schedule so the team can confirm coverage and prepare the next step.

Make qualifications visible beside the promise. If an inspection is required before pricing, say so. If an offer applies only to selected services or dates, explain the conditions where the offer appears. Do not hide a material limitation in a distant footer.

Give one action visual priority. A phone or WhatsApp option can remain available for customers who need clarification, but it should support the same enquiry. Keep useful business, contact and privacy information accessible instead of removing every link in pursuit of a supposedly perfect layout.

Answer the questions that decide whether the service fits

A service name rarely explains the scope. For office cleaning, buyers may need to know which spaces are covered, whether supplies are included, which schedules are possible and how access to the premises is arranged. The business must supply those answers; the writer should not invent them.

Use plain headings for inclusions, exclusions, coverage and the quotation process. If you publish a price, state its currency, basis and relevant conditions. If pricing depends on an assessment, explain the factors needed for a quote rather than inventing a “starting from” amount to fill the design.

For a Nigerian service business, location should be operationally useful. List confirmed coverage or explain how visitors check it. Do not claim nationwide service because the website is available nationwide. Where access arrangements, travel or delivery affect the offer, explain when those details are agreed.

Use evidence you can support

Choose material that helps a buyer assess the actual service: permissioned project photographs, an explained work process, relevant qualifications or an attributable customer comment. Explain what an image shows and why it matters. A general stock office photograph can illustrate a topic, but it should not stand in for a completed cleaning job.

Do not create testimonials, customer logos, review scores or “trusted by” counts. If the business is new, show the service scope, responsible team and a clearly labelled demonstration instead. A factual process explanation is more useful than an unsupported claim to be the best.

Keep proof close to the question it answers. An office-cleaning photograph belongs beside the service explanation; a short description of how quotations work belongs near the request button. Avoid making a visitor search an unrelated gallery for reassurance.

Worked example: an office-cleaning landing page

The following is original sample copy for a fictional business. It illustrates decisions, not a ready-to-publish offer. A real company must confirm every service, area and operational promise before using it.

Illustrative page outline for a Port Harcourt office-cleaning quotation
Page element Sample copy or content Reason for the choice
Headline Office cleaning for small workplaces in Port Harcourt Names the service, intended customer and location.
Supporting text Tell us your location, office size and preferred cleaning schedule. We will confirm coverage and discuss a quotation. Explains what the request starts without promising an instant price.
Scope section List approved inclusions, exclusions, supply arrangements and available schedules. Prompts the owner to provide facts rather than leaving the copywriter to guess.
Evidence Use an authorised workplace example with an accurate caption, or identify a demonstration as such. Shows relevant evidence without inventing a client endorsement.
Primary button Request an office-cleaning quote States the action and preserves the service context.
Expectation beside the form This request does not confirm a booking. We will discuss the scope and availability before agreeing the work. Makes the commitment clear before submission.

Design the form around the first useful conversation

Ask for the information needed to route and assess the enquiry. For this example, that might be a name, preferred contact method, office area, approximate size and cleaning schedule. Allow “not sure” where a precise measurement is unnecessary. Leave detailed access instructions and sensitive documents for an appropriate later conversation.

There is no universally correct number of fields. Remove questions nobody uses, but retain information needed to distinguish an appropriate request from one the team cannot serve. Keep the service selected when someone arrives from its page.

Use visible labels, clear required-field indicators and suitable input types. W3C's form-label guidance explains that controls need descriptive labels associated with them. Placeholder text disappearing as someone types is a poor substitute for a persistent instruction.

Show an honest result after submission. W3C's notification guidance calls for clear success feedback and errors people can resolve. Do not display “Request received” merely because the submit button was pressed. Agree with the developer what confirms acceptance and how a failed request can be retried.

Explain the intended use of the contact information and link to privacy information that reflects actual practice. Assign an enquiry owner and publish response hours or timing only if the team can meet them. A WhatsApp button also needs a responsible recipient; opening a chat is not proof that a message was sent.

Check mobile reading and performance before launch

Read the page on a phone using mobile data, not only a large design preview. Can you identify the service before the decoration? Does the request button remain understandable? Check text resizing, keyboard navigation, form errors and whether a sticky banner obscures useful content.

Use appropriately sized images with accurate descriptions. Avoid making visitors load a large background video to understand a simple service. Test the actual downloads and phone links. Check the destination of a WhatsApp link without sending a message.

Google's Web Vitals guidance distinguishes loading, interaction responsiveness and layout stability. Its good-experience thresholds are LCP at or below 2.5 seconds, INP at or below 200 milliseconds and CLS at or below 0.1, assessed at the 75th percentile of page loads. These are technical reference points, not conversion or ranking guarantees. A laboratory test helps diagnose problems; it does not establish every visitor's experience.

Measure useful enquiries separately from button clicks

Decide what you will record before buying traffic: visits, contact-button clicks, accepted enquiries, qualified requests and eventual customers. Define “qualified” using the service's real requirements, such as location and work type. Keep the original enquiry record so duplicate contacts do not become several supposed new opportunities.

Google Analytics lists generate_lead among its recommended events, with separate events for qualification and later outcomes. These require implementation; they are not proof that a working lead-tracking system already exists. Ask the developer to test when each event fires and prevent retries or repeated clicks from creating misleading totals.

Where you use campaign links, Google's URL-parameter guidance explains how source, medium and campaign values identify referring campaigns in configured analytics. Keep naming consistent and keep personal information out of URLs. Campaign tags do not independently verify an offline sale.

If visits produce enquiries outside the service area, review targeting and coverage copy together. If suitable enquiries arrive but nobody follows up, a different button colour will not fix the handover. Record a specific hypothesis and the change made; do not declare a winner from a handful of visits or compare periods without considering different traffic.

Run a final launch review with named owners

  • Business owner: confirms offer, coverage, evidence permissions, pricing conditions and achievable response expectations.
  • Writer and designer: check the advert-to-page match, scope, readable layout and primary action.
  • Developer: verifies links, mobile behaviour, accessible controls and form acceptance/error paths using safe test data and a test destination.
  • Enquiry owner: confirms where requests go, qualification criteria and how outcomes will be recorded.

Keep untested items visible in the review checklist. Never send a surprise live test enquiry or create a real booking to check the design. Complete agreed technical tests in a safe environment before directing campaign traffic to the page.
