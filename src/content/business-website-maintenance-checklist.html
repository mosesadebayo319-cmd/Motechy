A useful website maintenance checklist for a small business covers the customer journey, recovery, software updates, accurate content and account renewals. Give each check an owner and a clear pass condition. “The homepage opens” is a start; it does not tell you whether a buyer can download the right catalogue or whether an enquiry reaches the team.

Imagine an Ibadan furniture workshop whose website shows office desks and accepts quote requests. Its homepage looks fine, but the downloadable catalogue lists discontinued finishes and the enquiry form uses an old delivery account. The workshop needs a maintenance routine that follows how customers actually buy.

That workshop is a hypothetical example, not a MoTechy client or a report of measured losses. Use the editable website maintenance checklist and log to adapt the routine to your own site.

Know what you maintain and who owns it

Start with a short inventory: website address, domain registrar, hosting provider, content system, code repository, form delivery service, file storage and any payment or booking integrations. Record the responsible person and renewal date for each. Keep passwords and recovery codes in an appropriate password manager, not in the maintenance spreadsheet.

The work depends on the website:

  • WordPress: someone must maintain the core software, themes, plugins, database and uploaded files.
  • Managed website builder: confirm what the provider handles and what you must manage, including content, exports, integrations and account access.
  • Static or custom website: check the source, build tools, hosting configuration and connected services. A static page can still depend on a separate form endpoint.

Choose a business owner who can approve content and spending, and a technical owner who can investigate failures. One person may hold both roles. The important part is that an alert has a named destination and someone is authorised to act.

Set a routine without postponing urgent problems

Use these intervals as a starting plan, not a universal service standard. Increase checks where orders, bookings or frequent publishing make failures more costly.

  • Weekly: walk through the main customer route, review availability and backup alerts, and confirm current contact details.
  • Monthly: reconcile the full checklist, review updates and access, check important pages and downloads, and record the next actions.
  • After a change: retest affected pages and the enquiry or purchase route, even if the monthly review happened yesterday.
  • At an agreed recovery-test interval: restore a backup in an isolated environment and record the result. Choose the interval around data changes and business risk.
  • When an alert arrives: assess an outage, failed backup or relevant security advisory promptly; do not wait for the next calendar appointment.

Backup creation and monitoring may run automatically much more often than a person reviews this checklist. Agree the maximum acceptable data loss and interruption with your developer. A monthly review does not mean a monthly backup is sufficient.

Test the route from service page to usable enquiry

Open the site on a phone using mobile data, as well as on a desktop. Start with a service or product link a customer could receive in WhatsApp. Check the details, menu, call-to-action and download. Confirm that a phone link contains the right number and a WhatsApp link opens the intended account without sending a message.

On an enquiry form, inspect the selected service, labels, required fields and error messages. A successful-looking screen is not sufficient proof of delivery. Ask the developer to test acceptance and failure paths using a staging site and a test inbox or mocked provider. If live delivery must be checked, agree a controlled test with the recipient first; do not send surprise enquiries or create real orders as part of routine browsing.

Record what the test actually covered. “Form layout and validation passed; production delivery not tested” is useful evidence. “Everything works” hides the remaining uncertainty. Keep customer data out of screenshots and test fixtures.

Prove that a backup can restore the right things

Ask three questions: what is backed up, when did the latest successful backup finish, and what happened during the last restoration test? WordPress’s backup documentation explains that a typical full restore needs both database and files. A database export alone does not preserve uploaded photographs.

For a custom site, map the equivalent pieces: source code, uploaded media, database records and the configuration needed to run them. A Git repository may hold the application without holding its live orders. A host’s rollback feature may restore a deployment without restoring a separate database. Confirm the actual coverage with each provider.

Keep recoverable copies separately from the running site, with controlled access. In an isolated restore, check that important pages render, media opens and a representative workflow works. Prevent the restored copy from emailing customers or running live payments. Record the backup timestamp, restore date, checks and any missing data.

Choose backup frequency from the data the business cannot afford to recreate. If new enquiries are stored throughout the day, discuss the loss between backups explicitly. A “backup completed” notification confirms a job ran; a successful restore demonstrates something more useful.

Update software with a tested return path

Maintain a list of the software your site actually uses. For WordPress, that includes core, themes and plugins; for a custom application, it may include language runtimes, libraries and build tools. Assign someone to review relevant release notes and security notices.

WordPress recommends backing up before updating. For planned changes, have the technical owner verify recovery, test in a preview environment, apply the change and repeat the important customer checks. Agree who can restore service if the release fails, including how new data created after the backup would be preserved.

For projects using npm, its dependency audit documentation describes reports of known vulnerabilities and warns that some fixes can introduce breaking changes. Ask the developer to assess the advisory and test the remedy. A clean dependency report does not certify the whole website, and an automatic fix should not replace release testing.

Check accounts, renewals and the bills behind the site

Confirm that the business can access its domain and hosting accounts, with an appropriate second authorised person available if the main contact leaves. Review administrator access and remove permissions that are no longer required. Use individual accounts and multi-factor authentication where supported.

ICANN’s renewal guidance emphasises timely payment, current contact information and understanding the registrar’s terms. Record your actual provider’s dates and rules, including for a .ng domain. Do not assume another domain extension’s expiry or recovery timetable applies.

Check auto-renew status, payment method, billing currency and who sees failed-payment notices. For a Nigerian business paying a foreign-currency invoice, confirm that the chosen payment method can complete that provider’s charge. Do not treat an enabled auto-renew switch as proof of payment.

List email, forms, file storage and paid plugins separately if they renew separately. Check for HTTPS warnings and confirm who monitors certificate renewal. Routine maintenance should not unexpectedly move the domain, change email routing or replace hosting.

Review content, mobile reading and search evidence

Check your highest-value service pages, current offers, delivery areas, contact details and frequently downloaded files. For the furniture workshop, a catalogue revision is incomplete until the actual downloaded file contains the approved finishes and ordering instructions. Open the file; do not rely on its filename.

Try keyboard navigation and larger text. Look for visible focus, understandable labels and controls that remain usable on a small screen. The W3C accessibility Easy Checks are a useful starting point, but explicitly do not constitute a complete accessibility evaluation.

Repeat speed observations on comparable pages, devices and connections. Investigate an oversized new photograph or failing third-party script when the customer journey becomes noticeably slower. A single score is a diagnostic clue, not the business outcome.

With authorised access, review Search Console for unexpected changes in important pages. Google’s Search Console introduction explains indexing, search performance, security issues and page-experience reports. Record the affected URL and evidence before requesting a fix. Search clicks are not enquiries, and routine maintenance cannot promise rankings.

Worked example: one maintenance review for the workshop

Here is an illustrative review, with invented findings to show how to write useful tasks. It is not an audit of a real business. Each row turns a vague instruction into something another person can verify.

Illustrative maintenance log: owner, finding and acceptance evidence
Check and owner Example finding Evidence needed to close
Catalogue · business owner Downloaded PDF shows a discontinued finish. Approved replacement opens from the live product page and contains current options.
Enquiry route · developer Preview test reports success when the delivery provider rejects the message. Failure produces an honest retry message; an accepted request reaches the agreed test destination.
Recovery · technical owner Backup exists, but no restore evidence is recorded. Isolated restore loads product images and the sample workflow; timestamp and gaps are logged.
Renewal · account owner Hosting billing contact still names a former contractor. Business-controlled contact and payment readiness are confirmed in the provider account.
Mobile navigation · developer Large text makes the catalogue button overlap another control. The corrected layout remains readable and each control can be used independently.

Give each open item a due date based on impact. An unavailable site, suspected compromise or broken revenue path needs prompt escalation. An outdated optional team photograph can usually wait for planned content work. Keep unresolved items open rather than marking the month “done” because someone viewed every page.

Agree the maintenance scope before buying a plan

Ask what the plan includes, how frequently work happens, which evidence you receive and what happens outside support hours. Separate response time from resolution time. Confirm whether backups, restoration work, content edits, dependency updates and third-party charges are included or quoted separately.

The monthly handover can be one concise record: checks completed, changes released, evidence links, unresolved risks and the next owner and date. The maintenance log template provides those fields without requiring customer information.
