Social media management is usually priced around the work involved: planning, content production, publishing, community responses and reporting. Two proposals can both promise “12 posts per month” and describe very different services. One may include captions and graphics created from your existing photographs. Another may include travel, a shoot, video editing, interviews, comments and inbox coverage. Comparing the headline fee without those details can lead to an expensive misunderstanding.

If you are researching social media management costs in Nigeria, the useful question is what a monthly agreement will deliver and what you still need to provide. This guide gives you a way to compare proposals, work out the complete budget and decide whether a freelancer, agency or internal hire fits the job.

What should the management fee cover?

Ask the provider to describe the work from planning to review. The agreement should state who chooses topics, writes captions, supplies images, approves content, publishes it and handles responses. A list of platforms and post counts leaves most of that undefined.

Turn broad promises into a usable scope
Service What to clarify
Strategy and planning Research, content pillars, calendar frequency and who approves the direction
Writing and design Original concepts, captions, carousel slides, image sourcing and revision rounds
Video Whether the fee includes filming, editing supplied footage, subtitles or direction only
Publishing Platforms, formats, scheduling, adaptations and final checks
Community management Coverage hours, comments versus inboxes, response boundaries and escalation
Reporting Measures, reporting frequency, interpretation and agreed next actions
Advertising Campaign management, creative testing and media spend as separate items

Definitions matter. Is a five-slide carousel one post or five designs? Does a Reel use footage the client supplies? Does publishing to Instagram and Facebook count as one content item or two? Neither counting method is inherently wrong; ambiguity makes the comparison unreliable.

Why do social media quotes vary?

Production takes different amounts of work

A product photograph with a short caption and a filmed customer story have different production needs. Travel, location permissions, lighting, sound, editing and approvals can all change the budget. Ask which assets you will own and whether music, photography or stock licences impose restrictions.

More platforms can mean more than reposting

A provider may adapt a message for a professional audience, a vertical video or a visual carousel. Each version can need different writing, dimensions and review. If the plan simply republishes identical content, understand that before paying for a broader channel strategy.

Response coverage needs people and boundaries

Answering public comments twice a day is different from monitoring several inboxes throughout business hours. Define which questions the manager can answer, which require your sales team and how urgent complaints are escalated. Avoid promises of round-the-clock responses unless staffing and fees support them.

Approvals can expand the workload

If three stakeholders give contradictory feedback on separate days, a small deliverable can become a long production cycle. Appoint one client contact, consolidate feedback and agree an approval deadline. Specify how late information affects the publishing calendar.

Calculate the complete monthly budget

Use this formula: management + agreed production + advertising spend + required tools + applicable taxes or transaction charges. Confirm which amounts are included, additional or paid directly to a platform. Do not assume a management retainer is a media budget.

Illustrative budget, not a MoTechy quotation: management at ₦150,000, a separately scoped shoot at ₦60,000 and advertising spend of ₦90,000 produce a ₦300,000 subtotal. If the agreed services require other charges, add them separately. The ₦90,000 media allocation is not the manager’s fee.

For a fair comparison, send each provider the same brief: business goal, target audience, platforms, required formats, available assets, response coverage and approval process. Ask for a line identifying exclusions. A smaller quote may be a sensible choice when you can provide the missing work yourself.

Freelancer, agency or in-house manager?

A freelancer can be a good fit when the scope is focused and you want a direct working relationship. Clarify availability, specialist support and cover during absence. A capable freelancer may bring a strong skill set, but one person cannot provide unlimited production and response capacity.

An agency or studio can coordinate several disciplines when the work needs them. Ask who will actually manage the account, which work is subcontracted and how feedback reaches the people producing it. A larger team is useful only when its responsibilities are clear.

An in-house hire can build deep knowledge of products and daily operations. The employer also takes responsibility for salary, equipment, management, training and coverage. A salary figure cannot be compared directly with an external retainer without including the rest of the operating cost.

A mixed arrangement may fit best: your staff captures authentic product footage and answers detailed sales questions, while an external specialist handles planning, editing, publishing and analysis. Write those handovers into the scope.

How to judge value beyond follower growth

Define success before the first report. An awareness campaign, a training programme and a customer-support account have different jobs. Choose measures tied to that job, then include context such as the content published, spend, offer changes and response coverage.

  • For enquiries: track relevant conversations, qualified leads, response time and eventual outcomes where you can reliably connect them.
  • For awareness: review reach, relevant engagement and useful audience feedback, without describing views as customers.
  • For service: monitor unanswered questions, resolution time and issues passed to the right team.
  • For content: record which questions and formats produced useful responses, then explain what will change.

Ask for interpretation rather than a screenshot dump. If reach increased but suitable enquiries fell, the report should examine the audience, offer and next step. If a sale cannot reliably be attributed to a post, label that uncertainty instead of assigning it to social media by default.

What to agree before signing

  1. Ownership and access: the business retains ownership of its accounts and grants the access needed for the work. Use platform roles where available and multi-factor authentication.
  2. Deliverables: list formats, quantities, platforms, revisions and production responsibilities.
  3. Approvals: name the approver and agree when feedback must arrive.
  4. Response boundaries: define hours, complaint handling and who can discuss prices, refunds or sensitive matters.
  5. Reporting: agree the definitions, frequency and review meeting.
  6. Payment and exit: confirm fees, notice, outstanding work, asset handover and access removal.

Common pricing questions

Does management include advertising spend?

Only when the proposal explicitly says so. Ask for management and media spend to be shown separately, including who pays the platform and how the advertising account is owned.

Can a cheaper plan still be suitable?

Yes, when it covers a focused need and the responsibilities are clear. For example, a business with usable photographs and an internal sales team may need less external production and inbox support.

Can I guarantee a number of sales in the contract?

A provider can commit to agreed work and reporting. Sales also depend on the offer, price, demand, availability and your sales process. Treat an unconditional sales promise as something that needs evidence and precise conditions.
