# Service landing page review checklist

MoTechy · 10 November 2026

Companion guide: https://motechy.vercel.app/blog/landing-page-checklist-service-business

Copy this plain-text/Markdown template into your notes or spreadsheet. It is a
planning and acceptance checklist, not an automated audit or conversion promise.
Use actual approved business facts. Keep credentials and personal customer data
out of the review record.

Page-job sentence:

This page helps [customer] understand [offer] and take [action] as the next step.

## 2. Review content and evidence

- [ ] Headline names the actual service, intended customer and relevant location.
- [ ] Advert and page describe the same offer and next action.
- [ ] Inclusions, exclusions and coverage are approved by the business owner.
- [ ] Published prices have an approved currency, basis and relevant conditions.
- [ ] If assessment is needed before quoting, the process is explained.
- [ ] Material limitations appear beside the relevant promise.
- [ ] Primary button describes what pressing it will do.
- [ ] Useful business, contact and privacy information remains accessible.
- [ ] Photographs, testimonials and logos have permission and accurate context.
- [ ] Stock imagery and demonstrations are not presented as client work.
- [ ] No invented reviews, customer counts, prices, results or guarantees appear.

## 3. Hypothetical annotated example

Fictional Port Harcourt office-cleaning business. This is sample wording, not a
real offer or MoTechy client case. Confirm every detail before adapting it.

- Headline: Office cleaning for small workplaces in Port Harcourt.
- Supporting text: Tell us your location, office size and preferred cleaning
  schedule. We will confirm coverage and discuss a quotation.
- Scope: insert approved inclusions, exclusions, supply arrangements and schedules.
- Evidence: use authorised relevant work, or label a demonstration clearly.
- Primary button: Request an office-cleaning quote.
- Expectation: This request does not confirm a booking. We will discuss scope
  and availability before agreeing the work.

## 4. Form and follow-up acceptance

- [ ] Each question is needed for routing or the first useful conversation.
- [ ] Required fields are identified; optional fields are genuinely optional.
- [ ] A suitable contact method is requested without unnecessary duplication.
- [ ] Service selection survives the journey from page to form.
- [ ] Visible labels remain available after typing and are associated with controls.
- [ ] Keyboard operation, focus and understandable validation errors work.
- [ ] Success appears only after the agreed acceptance condition is met.
- [ ] Failure gives an honest explanation and a usable retry route.
- [ ] Privacy information reflects how contact details will actually be used.
- [ ] Named recipient, response expectations and qualification criteria are agreed.
- [ ] Safe test data and a test inbox/provider are available for technical tests.
- [ ] No surprise live enquiry, customer message or real booking is created.

Record exactly what was checked. A contact button click is not proof of enquiry
delivery. A chat opening is not a sent WhatsApp message. Mark missing tests as
“not tested”, not passed.

## 5. Mobile and measurement checks

- [ ] Page read on a phone using mobile data and on desktop.
- [ ] Large text, keyboard access and the primary action remain usable.
- [ ] Banners and sticky controls do not obscure form fields or service details.
- [ ] Images load at suitable sizes and have accurate descriptions.
- [ ] Downloads open with the expected contents; phone/chat destinations are correct.
- [ ] Loading, responsiveness and layout stability reviewed with appropriate tools.
- [ ] Metrics distinguish visits, clicks, accepted enquiries, qualified leads and customers.
- [ ] Event triggers verified using safe tests; retries/repeated clicks considered.
- [ ] Campaign naming consistent; URLs contain no personal information.
- [ ] Duplicate contacts are reconciled in business records.
