You don’t have a content problem. You have a strategy problem.

Most brands create more content than ever — and still can’t explain where their next client will come from. Volume isn’t the bottleneck. Direction is.

Content strategy · 9 min read By Moses Adebayo · MoTechy · Abuja

Walk into almost any SME conversation in Lagos, Abuja, or Port Harcourt and you’ll hear a version of the same complaint: “We’re posting consistently, but nothing is happening.” The feed is full. The team is tired. The DMs are quiet. Enquiries still come from “someone who knows someone.”

That frustration is real. What’s usually wrong is not effort. It’s the assumption that more content automatically equals more growth.

The content explosion made strategy optional — until it wasn’t

Businesses now publish across Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn, WhatsApp Status, and email — often without a single document that answers: who is this for, what should they do next, and how does this connect to revenue?

Marketing research and practitioner evidence keep pointing the same direction: content without an audience journey becomes decoration. Audiences skim; algorithms reward clarity and engagement quality, not mere frequency. Brands that treat every post as a business decision outperform brands that treat posting as a checkbox.

You don’t have a content problem. You have a strategy problem dressed up as a content calendar.

What “posting without strategy” looks like in practice

  • Pretty designs with no clear offer, audience, or next step.
  • Random topics based on trends instead of customer questions.
  • Hard sells every third post — then silence when nothing converts.
  • Metrics that feel busy (views, likes) with no path to calls or sales.
  • Copy-paste “motivational Mondays” that could belong to any competitor.

If a stranger lands on your page for three seconds, can they tell what you sell, who you help, and why you’re different? If not, content is failing its first job: positioning.

A simple definition of content strategy

Content strategy is the system that decides what you say, to whom, why, where, and what happens next — before design starts.

At MoTechy, every piece of content is supposed to do at least one of five jobs:

  • Educate (≈30%) — teach something useful; earn authority.
  • Prove (≈25%) — evidence, results, process, proof of work.
  • Engage (≈20%) — questions, debates, community.
  • Humanise (≈15%) — founder voice, behind the scenes, trust.
  • Convert (≈10%) — soft CTA, offer, book a call.

The ratio rule

For every 1 promotional post, publish at least 4 value posts. Reversing this ratio consistently damages trust — especially with sceptical Nigerian buyers who’ve seen too many empty promises online.

Start here: one question that rewires your calendar

Before you hire a designer or open Canva, answer this:

What is the ONE question your ideal client asks before they would ever hire you or buy from you?

Examples:

  • “How do I get customers without paid ads?”
  • “Why is my product not selling even though quality is good?”
  • “How long before content starts working?”
  • “What should I post if I’m a coach / fashion brand / B2B service?”

Write a clear, specific answer. Publish it. Build a week of content that orbits that answer. That single move outperforms a month of random aesthetics.

Map content to a journey (not a mood)

Think in three stages:

1. Awareness

They feel the problem but don’t know you. Content should name the problem better than they can.

2. Trust

They know you exist. Content should prove competence and reduce risk (process, proof, personality).

3. Decision

They’re close. Content should remove friction: pricing logic, who it’s for, how to start, FAQs, objections.

If 90% of your posts live only in “awareness vibes,” you’ll stay visible and broke. If 90% are hard sells, you’ll stay ignored.

What to measure (so strategy can improve)

Vanity metrics aren’t useless — they’re incomplete. Pair them with business signals:

  • Profile visits and website clicks after educational posts.
  • Saves and shares (people archive what they intend to use).
  • DM quality: “How much?” and “Can we talk?” beat “Nice post 🔥”.
  • Booked calls / WhatsApp enquiries attributed to content.
  • Repeat themes: which topics create conversations every time?

Then double down on signal and cut noise — the topic of our next insight.

A 60-minute strategy reset for busy founders

  • 0–15 min: Write your ideal client + the problem you solve in one sentence.
  • 15–30 min: List 10 questions they ask before buying.
  • 30–45 min: Assign each question a pillar (educate / prove / engage…).
  • 45–60 min: Plan 7 posts — one job each day — with one clear CTA for the week.

That’s a content strategy in embryo. Design becomes execution, not guesswork.
